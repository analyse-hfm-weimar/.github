# CAMAT · Computer-Assisted Music Analysis Toolbox

Computational music analysis and digital editions at the **University of Music
Franz Liszt Weimar**.

CAMAT brings **MEI**, **Python** and **Jupyter notebooks** into musicological
and music-theoretical research and education. This page connects the toolbox,
its documentation, teaching materials and the project's edition repositories.

## What can you do with CAMAT?

1. **Edit music with MEI.** Convert and assemble scores, connect notation to
   facsimiles, review encodings and run editorial checks.
2. **Parse musical representations.** Extract note and event tables, inspect
   piano rolls, and annotate selections while retaining links to source notes.
3. **Analyse music.** Explore distributions or search for motifs, chord
   progressions and textures using binary representations. Interpret matches
   through piano rolls and highlighted MEI notation.

Jupyter notebooks combine these tools with explanations and musical examples.
Our collaborator **[Jupyter4NFDI](https://nfdi-jupyter.de/)** provides browser
access to Jupyter, so researchers, lecturers and students can work without
installing a local Python environment. Follow the cloud guide to set up CAMAT
in the remote environment.

## Editions and corpora

The editorial work starts from page-level optical music recognition supplied
through the Bavarian State Library and **musiconn.scoresearch**. These encodings
contain recognition errors and require correction and review.

Current editorial projects:

- **[DdT I/11: Buxtehude's instrumental works](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_11)** —
  fourteen sonatas and three appendix works, edited by Carl Stiehl (1903).
- **[DdT I/29–30: Instrumentalkonzerte deutscher Meister](https://github.com/egorpol/DdT_1_vol_29_30)** —
  edited by Arnold Schering (1907), with works by Pisendel, Hasse, C. P. E. Bach,
  Telemann, Graupner, Stölzel and Hurlebusch.

See the [edition guide](https://camat-v2.readthedocs.io/en/stable/guides/edition-corpora/)
and individual repositories for their contents and editorial context.
